## 1. Executive Summary

Illicit cryptocurrency transactions have surged alongside the rapid expansion of multi-chain blockchain ecosystems. Ransomware operators, nation-state cybercrime syndicates (e.g., Lazarus Group), decentralized protocol exploiters, and money-laundering rings move billions of dollars across disparate ledger architectures annually. 

Despite blockchains being public ledgers, tracing illicit funds is increasingly hindered by sophisticated obfuscation layers:
- Multi-input/multi-output UTXO peel-chains in Bitcoin
- Privacy mixing pools (Tornado Cash, Wasabi Wallet)
- Collaborative transaction protocols (CoinJoin, PayJoin)
- Cross-chain asset bridges (Stargate, ThorChain)
- Rapid threshold-avoidance structuring and dormancy reactivation bursts

Existing commercial tools and academic models suffer from a fundamental flaw: **they rely on opaque, black-box scoring algorithms and heuristic guesswork**. When compliance officers or law enforcement agencies bring an investigation to court, single probability scores ("Risk: 87%") fail legal scrutiny under cross-examination because the underlying evidence, false-positive boundaries, and cryptographic chain of custody cannot be verified.

**Trazen (CryptoTrace)** was engineered to solve this exact problem: transforming raw, multi-chain transaction streams into **deterministic, explainable, and court-ready forensic intelligence dossiers** backed by immutable SHA-256 evidence ledgers.

## 2. Core Problem Dimensions

### A. Multi-Chain Architectural Divergence
- **Bitcoin (UTXO Model):** Transactions consume discrete unspent transaction outputs (`inputs`) and produce new outputs (`outputs`). In multi-input transactions, naive algorithms assume all inputs belong to the same entity (Common-Input Ownership Heuristic — CIOH), leading to catastrophic false-positive clustering when collaborative protocols (CoinJoin) are involved.
- **Ethereum (Account-Based Model):** Transactions represent state transitions between accounts (`from -> to`) governed by sequential nonces, contract internal calls, and ERC-20/ERC-721 token transfers. UTXO assumptions do not translate, requiring explicit distinction between direct EOAs, smart contracts, and bridge relays.

### B. Evasive Obfuscation Techniques
1. **Peel-Chains:** Attackers rapidly transfer funds through a sequence of single-hop wallets, peeling off small amounts while forwarding the bulk to evade fixed threshold surveillance.
2. **Privacy Mixers & Equal-Denomination Pools:** High-volume mixing pools sever the deterministic link between sender and receiver by pooling deposits and distributing standardized denominations.
3. **Structuring & Threshold Avoidance:** Splitting large illicit sums into multiple sub-reporting threshold tranches within brief temporal windows.
4. **Dormancy Reactivation:** Stashing stolen funds in idle wallets for months or years, followed by abrupt high-velocity liquidations.

### C. The Black-Box Scoring Dilemma
Most threat detection systems assign arbitrary risk scores generated by opaque machine learning models or static blacklist scraping. In a court of law:
- Investigators cannot state **why** a wallet received a specific risk score.
- Defense attorneys easily challenge arbitrary probability metrics that lack explicit deterministic rule attribution.
- False positives damage legitimate businesses and privacy-conscious users whose transactions inadvertently touched CoinJoin or shared exchange hot wallets.

### D. Evidentiary Chain of Custody & Precision
- **Precision Loss:** Standard JavaScript IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic introduces rounding errors when calculating multi-hop Satoshi (8 decimals) and Wei (18 decimals) balances.
- **Tampering Risk:** Digital evidence stored in standard databases lacks cryptographic provenance. An investigator must prove that ingested blockchain records, heuristic findings, and operator notes were not altered between initial query and court presentation.

## 4. Requirement Objectives for the Platform

1. **Problem Context & Awareness:** The user immediately understands the forensic problem, multi-chain challenges, and operational boundaries upon opening the platform.
2. **Investigation Workflow:** An unbroken 10-step investigation lifecycle from target selection to dossier export.
3. **Data Parity & Ingestion Flexibility:** Support for Live RPC Consensus Nodes, Imported CSV/JSON transaction batches, and Deterministic Offline Replay datasets.
4. **Graph Intelligence:** Directed NetworkX topology modeling on backend and Cytoscape.js interactive visualization on frontend.
5. **Deterministic Heuristics with False-Positive Safeguards:** Implement CoinJoin suppression, commercial exchange hub exclusion, and transparent confidence scoring.
6. **4D Explainability:** Replace single opaque risk numbers with four independent, bounded orthogonal dimensions.
7. **Tamper-Evident Evidence Vault:** SHA-256 hash-chaining of all ingested events and verification endpoints.
8. **Court-Ready Reporting:** Instant export of comprehensive Markdown and print-ready dossiers.
